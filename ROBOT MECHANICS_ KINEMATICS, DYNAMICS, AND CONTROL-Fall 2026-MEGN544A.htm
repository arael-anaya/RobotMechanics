Description

Mathematical representation of robot structures. Mechanical analysis including kinematics, dynamics, and design of robot manipulators. Representations for trajectories and path planning for robots. Fundamentals of robot control including, linear, nonlinear, and force control methods. Introduction to off-line programming techniques and simulation. Prerequisites: EENG307 and MEGN441, or grad standing. 3 hours lecture; 3 semester hours.

Robotics students should register for ROBO554 and all others for MEGN544.

Grading Policy

Grades will be calculated on the student's performance as described in the following table.

Course Activity Percentage of Grade (102%)
Participation (at least 20 out of 25 lectures) 2%
Homework Assignments (best 8 out of 10) 15%
Quizzes (best 8 out of 10) 15%
Programming Assignments 20%
Mid-term Exam (in class) 20%
Project (4 deliverables) 30%

Each activity is summarized below, and details of homeworks, quizzes, and the project can be found on our Canvas page.

Participation (2%)

Active participation is an essential part of this course. To encourage engagement and provide immediate feedback, we will use iClicker during lectures. Participation grades will be based on responding to in-class iClicker questions, regardless of whether the answers are correct. This ensures that you receive credit for being present, attentive, and involved in the learning process. Occasional absences are expected; thus 20% of missed lectures will be automatically excused.

Homework Assignments (15%)

Homework should be prepared with a type-setting program such as LaTeX or Word. Teamwork is allowed with a complementary report from each team member on their contribution to the homework. Your written work will be submitted online through Canvas. Please submit your homework as a PDF. All homework is due by midnight, no exceptions. Note that Canvas will mark your assignment late if you submit at 12:01 am. Your submission should represent the pride and professionalism you wish to demonstrate as a roboticist.  Neatness, organization, and clarity are essential skills for graduate students. 

Show all work clearly and neatly. Write out each step as if you were explaining your thought process to another person. It is always good practice to include a drawing and a problem statement.  A complete solution should include:

    1. Statement of your approach to solving the problem
    2. Governing equations
    3. Consistent problem-solving steps so your work can be followed
    4. Boxed or underlined final answers
    5. Assessment of answer (i.e., does it seem reasonable and why?)

You will be assigned 10 homeworks and for your grade I will consider the best eight.

Quizzes (15%)

Quizzes will take place on most Thursdays (see schedule for exact date and topic) at the end of the class. Every Quiz must be done by hand and independently. A calculator is only allowed for scalar arithmetic. 

After you finish your Quiz, you are responsible to: i) take a picture of it; ii) upload it on the Canvas assignment page; and ii) self-grade it. The solution of the Quiz and a rubric will be posted on Canvas after the class. Your grades will be reviewed and adjusted accordingly. 

You will be assigned 10 quizzes and I will consider the best eight for your final grade. 

Programming Assignments (20%)

There will be four programming assignments throughout the semester, all of which must be completed using MATLAB. These assignments are designed to help you apply course concepts, develop problem-solving skills, and gain proficiency in computational tools commonly used in engineering practice. A testing script will be provided for each assignment to help you verify the correctness and formatting of your solutions before submission. While the testing script is a valuable resource, it is not a substitute for thorough debugging and clear documentation of your code.

Mid-term Exam (20%)

You are allowed a graphing calculator (TI-89 equivalent or less) and 1 sheet (8.5x11") hand-written with notes/equations on both sides. The midterm will be held during our regular class period on October 16th. 

Project (30%)

The project is intended to advance your skill set from the in-class activities. You will complete a semester-long project in groups of 3–4. The project is intended to provide hands-on experience in applying course concepts to a larger, open-ended problem. Each group will be responsible for four deliverables:

  1. Project Proposal – One-page document (double-column IEEE format) outlining the problem statement, objectives, and approach. [20%]
  2. Mid-Review – One-page document (double-column IEEE format) presenting progress and receiving feedback. [15%]
  3. Final Report – Four-page document (double-column IEEE format) detailing the project outcomes. [60%]
  4. Individual Report – Summary (not more than one page) describing each team member’s specific contributions and responsibilities. [5%]

All group members are expected to contribute meaningfully, and both the quality of deliverables and the effectiveness of teamwork will be considered in grading. Late submissions will result in penalty of 10% per (24 hour) day.

Late Submission Policy for Homework Assignments Only

  • 10% off per (24 hour) day that the assignment is late.
  • Cases 1-3 do not require to give me a prior notice (although I very much appreciate it, when possible)
  • Cases 4-6 require to give me notice at least 1 week in advance
  • Case 7 is because sometimes unexpected things happen
    1. Religious holidays/observances
    2. Emergencies (crashed car, death in the family, house burned down, etc.)
    3. Unexpected Illness (note: I will require a note from your doctor if this becomes a frequent occurrence)
    4. Travel
    5. Athletic related activities
    6. Employment related activities
    7. Every student gets one "no questions asked" extension per semester 

Generative Artificial Intelligence

The Office of the Provost encourages the entire University community to explore the uses and impacts of GenAI technologies, whether through critical discussions or creative applications. Based on a review of the most recent Guidelines for Using Generative Artificial Intelligence at Colorado School of Mines, the GenAI policy for this course will be: By submitting work for evaluation in this course, you represent it as your own intellectual product. Submitting content for evaluation (e.g., ideas, text, code, images) that was generated, in whole or in part, by Generative Artificial Intelligence tools (including, but not limited to, ChatGPT and other large language models) would be considered Academic Misconduct in violation of Mines Academic Integrity/Misconduct Policy.

GenAI tools are not permitted for solving or completing homework or programming assignments in this course.
However, you are encouraged to use them as a resource for practicing problems, reviewing concepts,
or preparing for the mid-term exam, provided the work you submit for evaluation is entirely your own. This
distinction ensures that GenAI supports your learning process without compromising academic integrity.

Academic Integrity

Colorado School of Mines affirms the principle that all individuals associated with the Mines academic community have a responsibility for establishing, maintaining, and fostering an understanding and appreciation for academic integrity. In broad terms, this implies protecting the environment of mutual trust within which scholarly exchange occurs, supporting the ability of the faculty to fairly and effectively evaluate every student’s academic achievements, and giving credence to the university’s educational mission, its scholarly objectives, and the substance of the degrees it awards. We desire an environment free of any and all forms of academic misconduct and expects students to act with integrity at all times. Please read the full academic misconduct/integrity policy for full definitions of academic misconduct. Additionally, please use resources provided by the Office of Community Standards for guidance should you need to know more about the procedures of the policy for academic misconduct/integrity.

Expectations of online etiquette or netiquette

Here are few do's and don'ts about communicating in your course through emails or in online discussion forums:

  • Do…
    • Ask questions and engage in conversations as often as possible—feel free to contact the instructor via the discussion forum for questions or via email or other communication.
    • Be patient and respectful of others and their ideas and opinions they post online.
    • Remember to be thoughtful and use professional language. Keep in mind that things often come across differently in written text, so review your writing before posting.
    • Be prepared for some delays in response time, as "virtual" communication tends to be slower than "face-to-face" communication.
    • Contact the instructor if you feel that inappropriate content or behavior has occurred as part of the course.
    • Check the syllabus and course policies stated by your instructor to know what to expect about your instructor's turnaround time for responding.
  • Do NOT…
    • Use inappropriate language—this includes, but is not limited to, the use of curse words, swearing, or language that is derogatory.
    • Post inappropriate materials—for example, accidentally posting/showing a picture that is not appropriate for the course content.
    • Post in ALL CAPS, as this is perceived as shouting, and avoid abbreviations and informal language ("I'll C U L8R").
    • Send heated messages even if you are provoked. Likewise, if you should happen to receive a heated message, do not respond to it.
    • Send an email or post to the entire class, unless you feel that everyone must read it.
